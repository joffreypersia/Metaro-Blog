# Write for Metaro in English

Read the [editorial charter](../../../editorial/charte.md). Identify the reader, the decision they face, and the evidence that can help them make it. If the brief leaves the audience open, write for someone who works with images and is new to the specific technical issue.

## Give the reader something to work with

- Lead with the newsroom question, finding, or action. In a how-to, name the expected result and show how to check it. In an analysis, lead with the question the evidence can answer. For a caption, start with the scene and the verified facts.
- Build the explanation around a file, a field, or a consequence at the picture desk. Explain what a reader should see after each consequential step. Label hypothetical examples and keep reported observations tied to their source.
- Use the verbs people use at work. “Open the delivered JPEG and check its credit line” gives the reader a step they can take. Preserve specific details and useful differences in pace when editing an author's draft.

## Write for an international readership

- Default to US spelling and sentence-case headings unless the brief gives another house style. Spell out the month when a date could be misread. Use “you” for instructions and a reported voice for coverage.
- Prefer the terms a picture desk uses, such as “caption,” “credit line,” and “picture editor.” Name the exact property or tag when the distinction matters. Keep verified software labels, commands, field names, and values verbatim.
- Cut marketing claims and generic scene setting. Define acronyms on first use if the intended readers need the definition. Use the same term when discussing the same technical concept across a piece.
- When adapting a French draft, rebuild the lead and sentences in natural English. Carry over the sources, factual qualifications, credits, and rights language. Mark translated direct quotes and preserve the source wording in the editorial notes when available.

## Hand off the draft

Read the lead and headings as a sequence. They should explain what the reader will learn or do. Apply the shared review to captions and substantive claims before presenting the copy as ready for publication. Put unresolved questions in an editorial note. [Metaro editorial review](../metaro-editorial-review/SKILL.md)

Example edit. “Robust metadata practices are essential in today's media landscape” becomes “Check the credit line in the JPEG you deliver. That's the file the picture desk will receive.”
